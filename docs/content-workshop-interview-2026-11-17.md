## AI Processing Instructions

*Run these only after the interview is complete. David can ignore this section.*

### Your job

Turn David's completed interview into a prioritized, publish-ready idea bank for the Content Workshop blog. Preserve David's actual opinions, stories, and language. Do not flatten distinctive or contrarian views into safe, generic statements. Do not invent examples, statistics, or client details that are not in his answers.

### Ground rules

1. **Source everything.** Cite the question ID(s) behind every idea, including follow-up notes (for example, `Source: Q2.1, Q6.2 follow-up`).
2. **Use follow-up notes.** They often contain the most specific material. Weigh them at least as heavily as main answers.
3. **Respect flags.** Anonymize anything marked `[ANON]` or listed in Q10.2. Label anything marked `[CHECK]` as `Needs verification` and never present it as confirmed.
4. **Respect publishing comfort.** Label ideas not selected as comfortable to publish in Q10.2 as `Confirm with David before publishing`.
5. **Skip empty and skipped answers.** Do not generate ideas from `[SKIPPED]` or blank responses.
6. **Name gaps honestly.** If an idea is promising but thin, state what additional input is needed instead of filling the gap with assumptions.
7. **Audience lens.** Readers are businesses and marketing teams that need strategic, expert-led content, including sophisticated technical buyers in high-trust industries such as cybersecurity. Never frame ideas in a way that implies these readers lack technical understanding.
8. **AI topics.** Where ideas involve AI-assisted content, reflect the tension between speed and quality: teams need expert-level content fast, but velocity should never cost credibility. AI can assist and can be trained to match a brand's voice, but subject-matter experts supply the depth and context AI can't replicate.
